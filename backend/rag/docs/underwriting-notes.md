# Underwriting Notes

General underwriting guidance for qualifying borrowers. These notes describe how
we assess a file; they do not set rates.

## Debt-to-income

- The debt-to-income ratio compares total monthly debt payments to gross monthly
  income.
- Conventional loans generally target a back-end ratio at or below 45%, with
  flexibility up to 50% when strong compensating factors exist.
- Government loans allow higher ratios when residual income and credit history
  support the file.

## Credit

- We use the middle of three credit scores; for multiple borrowers, the lowest
  middle score usually governs pricing and eligibility.
- Recent late payments, collections, and derogatory events require a written
  explanation and may require a waiting period.
- Bankruptcies and foreclosures carry program-specific seasoning requirements
  before a borrower is eligible again.

## Income and assets

- Employment and income must be documented and stable, typically with a two-year
  history for variable or self-employment income.
- Down payment and reserve funds must be sourced and seasoned; large deposits
  require explanation.
- Reserve requirements increase for investment properties and multiple financed
  properties.

## Compensating factors

When a file is marginal, compensating factors such as low loan-to-value,
significant reserves, a long stable job history, or minimal payment shock can
support an approval that would otherwise be declined.
